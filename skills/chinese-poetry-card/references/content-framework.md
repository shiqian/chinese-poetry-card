# Content framework

The five images form one lesson, but each has a different job:

封面：让人知道今天学哪首诗、哪个成语
诗歌卡：诗里写了什么？
诗人卡：写诗的人是什么样的人？
背景卡：诗句背后有什么新知识？
成语卡：这个意思今天怎么说、怎么用？

## Writing rules

- 诗歌卡: use the complete verified poem and a short natural white-language meaning. Do not explain historical terms here.
- 诗人卡: merge biography and one unusual, sourced distinction into one readable passage. Include dates, identity/place when reliable, literary identity, and a brief style note. Prefer concrete wording children can understand over labels such as 仕途不显.
- 背景卡: choose only non-obvious facts that help a parent explain the poem: calendar, place, people, objects, customs, soundscape, or literary convention. Each item should not repeat the poem meaning.
- 成语卡: select a genuinely related idiom suitable for ages 4–8. Prefer concrete or imageable idioms; define it in spoken Chinese and give one or two examples from family, school, nature, or play. Avoid rare or allusion-heavy idioms unless the lesson explicitly teaches the allusion.

Content should be specific, memorable, and source-supported—not a list of standard textbook facts, generic praise, stereotypes, or unverified anecdotes. Keep a research record for every non-obvious claim.

## Poet identity brief

Before generation, define age impression, facial features, hair/headwear, clothing silhouette and colors, temperament, pose language, and historically suitable setting. Use the same identity on the cover and first three cards; vary action and framing, never the person's identity.
