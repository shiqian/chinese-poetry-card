# Content framework

The Yueke reference set establishes the reusable combination pattern for every new poem:

```text
诗歌学习卡：先让孩子读懂诗句
诗人简介卡：再认识写诗的人
诗歌背景卡：补充诗句之外的新知识
成语学习卡：把诗意迁移到今天的生活
```

Reuse this structure and information hierarchy, not the Yueke-specific wording or facts. Every new poem needs its own verified person, setting, background, and related idiom.

## Poet identity continuity

Before image generation, define the poet's visual identity separately from the layout reference. The identity brief should describe the poet's age impression, face, hair or headwear, clothing, temperament, and historically suitable environment. Apply it consistently to the cover and the three classical cards. A reference poet's appearance must never be copied merely because the reference image is visually convenient.

## 诗歌学习卡：读懂诗

Write a short, natural paraphrase of the complete poem. Mention the visible action and emotional situation, but do not explain why a season, place, object, or custom has its historical meaning. Those belong on the background card.

## 诗人简介卡：记住这个人

Use one integrated passage, not separate `简介` and `冷知识` sections. Recommended order:

1. name, dynasty, approximate dates, and place;
2. one memorable identity or life contrast;
3. one sourced phrase or contemporary judgment that makes the character vivid;
4. literary group, representative work, and a short description of poetic style.

Prefer concrete child-friendly language over labels such as “仕途不显”. For example, explain a status contrast as “长期只是地方小官” when the sources support it. Avoid overclaiming “一生” unless the evidence is secure.

## 诗歌背景卡：补足诗外知识

Select only details that are useful for understanding the poem and not obvious from a first reading. Explain terms, places, people, objects, customs, dates, and literary conventions. Each row should answer a likely parent question and have a source note in the research record.

## 成语学习卡：迁移到孩子生活

The target learner is 4–8 years old. Choose an idiom that genuinely connects to the poem's mood, action, or central image and that a young child can understand and eventually use.

- Prefer familiar, concrete, imageable idioms such as `一望无际` and `夜深人静`.
- Avoid rare, highly abstract, or allusion-heavy idioms such as `曲高和寡`, `囊萤映雪`, and `沧海桑田` unless the lesson is explicitly designed as a separate allusion introduction.
- Write the definition in everyday spoken Chinese.
- Use examples from family, school, nature, play, and ordinary child life.
- If a slightly harder idiom is educationally valuable, pair it with a very clear illustration and a short explanation rather than assuming the child already knows the allusion.

The idiom card may be visually different in its scene, but it must remain quiet, printable, and compatible with the series.

## Series-level writing rule

The four cards should feel like four chapters of one lesson, not four versions of the same explanation:

```text
诗歌卡：诗里写了什么？
诗人卡：写诗的人是什么样的人？
背景卡：诗句背后有什么知识？
成语卡：这个意思今天怎么说、怎么用？
```
