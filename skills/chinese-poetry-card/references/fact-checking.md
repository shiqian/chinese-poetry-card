# Fact-checking reference

## Source priority

Prefer, in order:

1. the poem's reliable textual edition or a recognized classical-text database;
2. primary historical sources, local gazetteers, collected works, poetry criticism, and contemporaneous prefaces;
3. modern academic research from university presses, museums, libraries, or scholarly institutions;
4. reputable institutional summaries for orientation only.

Do not treat an unsourced encyclopedia sentence as proof of an anecdote. When an image includes a memorable claim, keep the exact source and wording in the working notes.

## Common cautions

- Use `约` for dates when sources differ.
- Distinguish “宋太祖后裔” from the more specific “宋太祖八世孙”; the latter may be a traditional or later genealogical formulation and should not be presented as a direct quotation from the main body of the `宋史` without verification.
- Distinguish `酬倡` from a modern paraphrase such as “酬唱诗”; preserve the source wording when quoting.
- Distinguish the poet's official post, location, and date; do not infer a precise itinerary from a later summary.
- Distinguish a poet's style, collection title, studio name, and cultural source. For example, for Yang Wanli: `诚斋` is the studio name, `《诚斋集》` is the collection, and `诚斋体` is the poetic style; if citing the source, quote `《中庸》：“诚者，天之道也；诚之者，人之道也。”` accurately.

## Working research record

Before generation, record each non-obvious fact as:

```text
claim: <fact used in the card>
source: <work, author, volume or chapter, and URL or edition>
status: primary / later record / modern scholarly conclusion / disputed
card_use: poet / background / omitted
wording: <safe wording for the image>
```

Only the safe wording goes into the image. Keep source details outside the card unless the user explicitly requests citations on the card.
